How a sphere swims by moving its surface

A squirmer is the classic model of a ciliated microorganism: a sphere whose surface slides backward, pushing it forward. Slide β to morph it from a pusher into a puller, switch frames, and steer it with your cursor. Every tracer moves with the exact Stokes-flow solution.

What you are seeing

Each tracer is a fluid particle carried by the flow and colored by its speed. In the lab frame the fluid far away is at rest: as the squirmer passes, nearby tracers are pushed aside and then drawn back, tracing loops instead of being swept along. In the swimmer frame you ride along with the squirmer and watch the fluid stream past it.

The surface of the sphere slides with velocity

us(θ)= B1sin⁡θ (1+βcos⁡θ) , U=2B13

and it swims at speed U = 2B1/3 no matter what β is. The second mode, β = B2/B1, does not change the speed at all. It decides how the swimmer disturbs the fluid. In the lab frame, with θ measured from the swimming direction, the flow is

ur= 23B1 a3r3 cos⁡θ+ B2 ( a4r4− a2r2 ) P2(cos⁡θ) uθ= 13B1 a3r3 sin⁡θ+ B2 a4r4 sin⁡θcos⁡θ

The B2 term decays slowly, like 1/r2. That is the flow of a force dipole, and its sign separates two kinds of swimmers. Pushers (β < 0), like E. coli, are propelled from behind and push fluid out along their axis. Pullers (β > 0), like the alga Chlamydomonas, pull themselves forward and draw fluid in along their axis. A neutral squirmer (β = 0) leaves only a source dipole that fades like 1/r3. When |β| > 1 the surface slip reverses near one pole, which you can spot in the moving surface dots.

Steering, honestly

When you steer, the swimmer is turned by an external torque, the way a magnetic field turns a magnetic microrobot. A rigid rotation adds a rotlet flow, Ω × r (a/r)3. Because Stokes flow is linear, adding it to the squirming flow is exact. The flow is also axisymmetric about the swimming direction, so fluid in any plane through that axis stays in the plane. The canvas shows exactly that plane.

Why reciprocal strokes go nowhere

At low Reynolds number, the flow responds instantly to the boundaries, with no memory and no coasting. A stroke that retraces itself backward, like a scallop opening and closing, undoes its own progress exactly. This is Purcell's scallop theorem. Pick Reciprocal: the arms move in step, the loop in shape space collapses to a line, and the center returns to the same spot every cycle, to machine precision.

The four-step stroke of Najafi and Golestanian breaks the symmetry. Shrink arm 1, shrink arm 2, extend arm 1, extend arm 2: the path in the (L1, L2) plane encloses an area, and for small strokes the net displacement per cycle is proportional to that area. Swimming is geometry.

Screening in a porous medium

The three-sphere swimmer moves only because each sphere feels the flow created by the other two. In a porous medium, modeled by the Brinkman equation, that long-ranged hydrodynamic interaction is screened over a distance 1/α. Slide the screening up and the swimmer slows down. We found the same effect in our study of this swimmer in a Brinkman medium, where speed and efficiency both drop, in contrast to undulatory, helical, and squirmer-type swimmers, which are enhanced.

Notes on the models

The squirmer flow is the exact Stokes solution for a sphere with the first two squirming modes (Lighthill 1952; Blake 1971), plus a rotlet when the swimmer is turned. Tracers are integrated with a midpoint rule. The three-sphere swimmer uses point-force hydrodynamic interactions between the spheres, Oseen-level in a clear fluid and the Brinkman fundamental solution in a porous medium, with the sphere drag corrected for Brinkman screening. This captures the mechanism and the trends, not the full resolved calculations in the papers. Everything runs in plain JavaScript, with no libraries and no data sent anywhere.
